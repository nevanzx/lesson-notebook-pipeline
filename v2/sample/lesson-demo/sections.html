1  Fixed vs. Variable Cost

Feasibility starts with one honest question: which costs move when the cart sells more, and which sit still no matter what? Get this wrong and every later number is wrong.

Definition

Fixed cost does not change with the number sold. Variable cost rises and falls with every plate.

Once you have sorted them, keep the table beside you — it is the spine of the lab.

2  The Feasibility Gate

A break-even number alone does not make a venture feasible. A cart can break even on paper and still die from a weak market or thin skills. Judge each domain, then decide go or no-go — the way the lesson does.

3  The Break-Even Lab

Here is the formula you will feel with the sliders, not just memorise:

BEP (units) = Fixed Cost ÷ (Price − Variable Cost per unit)
The Price − VC part is the contribution margin — what each plate contributes to paying off the fixed cost.

Try the presets first. Then break the plan on purpose: drag the price below the variable cost and watch the lab refuse to find a break-even.

5  Where the Method Fails

Break-even analysis is a lamp, not a sun: it lights a small circle very well and leaves the rest dark. Learn to spot the dark.

Assumes a flat price. Volume discounts quietly change the margin.
Assumes all output sells. BEP is not the same as demand.
